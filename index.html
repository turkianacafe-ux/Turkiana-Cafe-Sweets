STORIES BEHIND THE FLAVORS

The Coffee That Traveled Centuries

"Every cup of Turkish coffee tells a story of caravans, sultans, and moonlit conversations."

In the 16th century, when coffee first arrived in Istanbul from Yemen, it was served in the grand halls of Topkapi Palace. Our Turkish coffee recipe is a direct descendant of those royal brews, perfected over 500 years of Ottoman tradition.

The foam, known as 'köpük', is considered the soul of the coffee. In Turkish tradition, the thickness of the foam predicts fortune - the thicker it is, the brighter the future. Our baristas have mastered this ancient art, creating perfect foam every time.

Mehmet Bey

Third-generation coffee master

The Baklava That Crossed Empires

"Layers of history, sheets of tradition, nuts of prosperity - each bite connects you to Ottoman grandeur."

Our baklava recipe comes from Gaziantep, the UNESCO Creative City of Gastronomy. The technique of layering paper-thin phyllo dough was perfected in the kitchens of the Topkapi Palace, where baklava was considered food of the gods.

Every November, during the Ottoman era, the Sultan would hold the 'Baklava Procession' where trays of baklava were carried from the palace to the Janissaries. Our pistachios are still sourced from the same Antep region, known for producing the world's finest.

Fatima Hanım

Baklava artisan from Gaziantep

The Breakfast That Became a Ritual

"A Turkish breakfast isn't a meal - it's a three-hour conversation with food as the mediator."

The tradition of 'kahvaltı' (literally 'before coffee') dates back to Ottoman times when families would gather on Sunday mornings for elaborate spreads. Our breakfast platter includes 17 different items, each with its own story.

The white cheese comes from the Aegean coast, the olives from the groves of Gemlik, the honey from the flower fields of the Black Sea, and the tea from the misty plantations of Rize. Each element is a taste of Turkey's diverse geography.

Zeynep Hanım

Breakfast tradition keeper
